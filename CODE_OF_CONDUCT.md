# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to build something, not to waste
your time.

## What is expected

- **Argue about the work, not the person.** "This breaks resumability because
  the state is no longer JSON-encodable" is a review. "This is amateurish" is
  not.
- **Say why.** A rejected suggestion with a reason teaches; one without it
  reads as authority.
- **Assume competence and missing context**, in that order. Most disagreements
  here are two people holding different halves of a design decision.
- **Accept that maintainers say no.** A package that accepts every feature
  becomes one nobody can reason about. A no should come with a reason, and often
  with a better place for the idea.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, personal attacks, discrimination, sexualised language or imagery,
publishing someone's private information, and sustained disruption of
discussions. This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, code and any space where you are representing this project.

## If something goes wrong

Report it privately to the maintainers through GitHub — open a
[private security advisory](https://github.com/v1j4yk/agentic_flutter/security/advisories/new)
if you would rather the report not be public anywhere; it is the private channel
this repository has, and it will be read.

Reports are handled in confidence. Depending on severity, the response is a
private word, a public correction, removal of the content, or a block. The
maintainers' decision is final, and if you are the subject of one you will be
told what it was and why.

Maintainers are held to this more strictly than contributors, not less.

## Scope

This applies to everyone taking part: contributors, maintainers, and people
opening their first issue.
